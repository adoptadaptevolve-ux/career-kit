# Phase 6: After the call

## Reflection

Ask four questions, one at a time. Do not analyze until all four are answered.

1. What actually happened? Which questions did they ask?
2. Which answer felt most natural, and why that one?
3. What did you assume about them, the role or the team that turned out wrong?
4. One thing you would change for the next round.

Then capture: what the interviewer said about the process, timeline, next interviewer, and any numbers. Record the user's self-score and the reason. Score recalled answers on the six delivery checks from Phase 5. Offer to add new facts or stories to the Candidate Profile.

## Follow-up emails

Two different emails. Never send the recruiter and the interviewer the same text.

**Confirmation to the recruiter** (within the hour, same thread, keep the subject)
- 2 to 4 sentences: thanks, the exact date, time, time zone and interviewer name, thanks for any resource sent.
- At most one easy-to-skip question, or none.
- Do not restate interest in the role.

**Thank-you to the interviewer** (same afternoon or next morning)
- Write it after the call, never before; a pre-written note cannot refer to anything that was said. A skeleton with bracketed blanks is fine to prepare.
- Under about 120 words: a one-line thanks, one specific callback to something the interviewer said, one concrete link to the candidate's real experience, then stop.
- Optional: a sharper one or two sentence answer to a question they fumbled. Never ask for anything.
- Fill every blank with a true detail. Mark unknowns `[confirm]`.

**Sound human**
- Vary punctuation and rhythm: a short sentence, a contraction, a question. Flat text reads as machine-written.
- Cut: "I hope this finds you well", "thrilled", "excited for the opportunity", "not only X but Y", three-adjective lists, "moreover", "leverage", "passionate about", "please don't hesitate", a closing paragraph that restates fit.
- Apply the user's writing preferences from the profile.
- Read it aloud. If the user would not say it, change it. If the user rewrites a draft, keep their wording and fix only grammar.

## Next round

When another interview is booked, update the prep instead of rebuilding it.

1. Read the invitation email: interviewer name, exact title, duration, date, time zone. Reclassify the interviewer.
2. Capture the finished round (reflection above).
3. Say what changed: what this interviewer decides and which earlier assumptions are now wrong.
4. Plan: the 2 or 3 stories to lead with, the tools and numbers to have cold, likely probes, the honest gap line.
5. Ask at most 3 questions for facts the profile lacks. Mark unanswered items `[confirm]`.
6. If a study deck exists, add a tab for this round instead of rebuilding it (see `study-deck.md`).
7. Draft the recruiter confirmation now. Leave the thank-you as a bracketed skeleton for after the call.

## Tracking (optional)

If the user keeps an application log, offer a one-line entry: date, company, role, round, interviewer, outcome, biggest risk question.
