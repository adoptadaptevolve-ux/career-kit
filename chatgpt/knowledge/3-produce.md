# Phase 3: Produce the better version

Goal: write the material. Phase 1 said what is wrong; Phase 2 said what the job wants. This phase turns both into text the candidate can send.

Contents: 3a Keyword placement map · 3b Bullet rewrites · 3c Tailored resume · 3d Summaries · 3e LinkedIn rewrite · 3f Interview answer pack

Every mode follows the ground rules in SKILL.md: never invent, keep every number with its employer, place only keywords the experience supports, quote the original line when changing it, and apply the user's writing preferences.

If Phase 1 or 2 findings exist in the conversation, use them as the brief instead of re-judging.

---

## 3a. Keyword placement map

**Needs:** resume and JD.

1. **Already present:** each keyword with where it appears.
2. **Missing, by weight:** ordered by how heavily the JD stresses them.
3. **Can be added honestly:** the subset the experience supports. Anything the user cannot claim goes to **Genuine gaps (do not add)**.
4. **Placement map**

| Keyword | Where to add | Draft wording | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|---|

Evidence status: `In resume (implied)`, `Confirm with user`, or `Gap`. Draft wording is a complete, true sentence or a clause in an existing bullet, never a bare list of terms.

Check literal variants: spelled-out and acronym forms, singular and plural, the JD's exact wording over a synonym the user prefers.

## 3b. Bullet rewrites

**Needs:** resume, target role.

Structure each line as Action, Task, Result, Business impact, in one or two lines: *strong verb + what and scope + how (tools) + measurable result + why it mattered*. Drop pieces the profile cannot support rather than filling them with plausible guesses.

Per bullet:
- **Before:** the original, quoted
- **After:** the rewrite
- **Status:** `Supported`, `Inferred, confirm`, or `Needs metric`
- **Question:** only for `Needs metric`; one specific question, e.g. "How many tickets a day did the queue handle?"

Start with the two most recent roles and the bullets closest to the target. Shorten older or unrelated roles rather than rewriting them. Keep tool names exactly as the user wrote them.

---

## 3c. Tailored resume

**Needs:** resume, JD, Phase 2 decode (run it first if missing).

Rewrite by reordering and rewording what is already true. Then list what changed and why in three to six lines.

**ATS-safe layout:** single column; standard headings (Summary, Skills, Experience, Education, Certifications); reverse-chronological; plain bullets; one consistent date format; no tables, text boxes, icons, photos, or content in headers and footers, because many parsers drop or scramble them. Put the target's keywords in Skills and in the experience lines that really show them.

If the user wants a file, build it with the docx skill when available, using this layout.

## 3d. Summaries

**Needs:** resume, JD.

Five options of three to four sentences, each with a short angle label (for example *Technical depth*, *Delivery and impact*, *Domain fit*, *Ownership*, *Career-change bridge*). Pick angles that fit the candidate's real strengths. Each covers title and years, the strongest matching skills, one proof point from the profile, and the value to this team.

No first-person pronouns (resume convention). Finish by recommending one and saying why in a sentence.

---

## 3e. LinkedIn rewrite

**Needs:** resume or profile, target role. If Phase 1 ran a `linkedin-audit`, use its findings as the brief.

- **Headline:** three options under 220 characters. Role and searchable terms first, a differentiator after.
- **About:** the first two or three lines carry the weight because LinkedIn truncates there. Open with something concrete about the work, not an adjective. First person is normal. Close with what they are looking for or how to reach them.
- **Experience:** per role, a two to three line context sentence plus a few bullets. Titles, employers and dates must match the resume exactly; mismatches are a screening red flag.
- **Skills:** about 15 to 30, ordered by what the target role searches for, with three to pin. Drop skills the user cannot back up.
- **Extras:** Featured section ideas (best project, article, repo) and a clean custom URL.

Write it to sound like this particular person: contractions where natural, varied sentence length, no hype words. Recruiters read many AI-written profiles and distrust them.

## 3f. Interview answer pack

**Needs:** resume, JD. (For a full staged interview prep, use Phase 5 instead. This is the quick written version.)

Twenty likely questions, adjusted to the role: about 5 role or technical, 4 behavioral, 3 probing weak spots (gaps, short stints, missing requirements), 3 project deep-dives, 3 motivation or situational, 2 logistics (availability, pay, notice).

For each:
1. **What the interviewer is testing**
2. **Answer structure** (STAR, claim-evidence-tradeoff, or situation-approach-result)
3. **Sample answer** of 80 to 120 spoken words, built only from the profile. When the profile has no material, write `Story needed:` and what to dig up.
4. **Common mistake to avoid**

Deliver ten at a time, or offer to save the full set as a file.
