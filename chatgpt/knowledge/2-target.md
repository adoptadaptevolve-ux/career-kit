# Phase 2: Target a job

Goal: work out what the employer actually wants, and how well the candidate already covers it. Phase 3 writes the better version; this phase decides what it needs to say.

**Needs:** the JD (required), the resume or Candidate Profile.

## Output, in this order

1. **Role in one sentence:** the problem this hire exists to solve, in plain words.

2. **Decode table**

| Category | Item | JD evidence (short paraphrase) | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|

Categories:
- *Key skills*
- *Responsibilities* (note any percentage split the JD gives)
- *Hiring goals*: the pain behind the posting, often inferred from words like "scale", "migrate", "own", "greenfield". Label these as inferences.
- *ATS keywords*: exact strings plus common variants and acronyms, for example "Amazon Web Services (AWS)".
- *Standout traits*: what would separate a strong candidate from an adequate one.

Priority is Must, Should or Nice, judged by how often and how early the JD stresses it. Keep required and preferred visibly separate when the JD separates them.

3. **Coverage (gap analysis)**

| JD requirement | Candidate evidence (from the profile) | Coverage |
|---|---|---|

Coverage is Full, Partial or Missing. Quote or point to the evidence. Never mark Full without it.

4. **Honest read:** two or three sentences on fit. Is this a reach, a match, or a step down? What is the single biggest risk?

5. **Gaps the user must close:** for each Missing or Partial requirement, one of: address it in a cover letter or summary, ask the user about unlisted experience, or accept it as a real gap. Add these to the running gap list.

Then offer Phase 3.
