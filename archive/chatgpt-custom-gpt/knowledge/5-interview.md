# Phase 5: Interview prep

Goal: a staged prep that covers what a recruiter, hiring manager or technical interviewer will probe, without over-studying. The whole sequence takes about 45 minutes. If it is late the night before, say that sleep beats another round.

**Run one stage at a time.** One stage, one output, then move on. Dumping every stage into one reply produces a wall nobody reads.

## Inputs to collect first

Ask for anything missing in one message.

1. The JD. Read every line; key responsibilities and any percentage split drive the fit map.
2. Company website (search for it if not given).
3. Interviewer name and title. **Take the title from the invitation or scheduling email, not from a spoken recap**; a recap can get the function wrong, and that changes the whole prep. Classify the interviewer: recruiter, staffing or services intermediary, hiring manager, or technical peer.
4. Date, format (virtual or on-site), round (screen, panel, final), duration.
5. If an earlier round happened: what was asked, what landed, next steps, whether pay came up.
6. The Candidate Profile. It is the only fact source. If a story needs a detail the profile lacks, mark it `[confirm]` and give a fallback.

## Stages

| Stage | Output |
|---|---|
| 1. Company cheat sheet | One page on the company and how the candidate lines up |
| 2. Gap check | Top 3 gaps with honest one-liners |
| 3. Story bank | 3 to 4 stories as beats, not scripts |
| 4a. Scorecard and question bank | Competencies, questions, probes |
| 4b. Live mock | 5 questions in character, debrief after "done" |
| 5. Talking points and questions | 3 points to raise, 3 questions to ask |
| 5b. Pay | Beats for the compensation question |
| 7. Final rundown | One scannable page |
| (optional) Study deck | See `study-deck.md` |

Stage 6 (reflection and follow-up) is in `6-after.md`.

## Stage 1: Company cheat sheet

Run 6 to 10 targeted searches, primary sources first (company site, press releases, regulator sites, LinkedIn company page). Cover: what it sells and to whom, size, stage, funding, leadership, recent news (last 18 months), customers, published results (flag vendor-reported), values in the company's own words, product vocabulary, competitors, tools named in the JD, and any intermediary firm. Search "<company> interview questions" for recurring questions and say plainly if none exist. Flag conflicting figures.

Return one page:
1. What they do, in 5 sentences or fewer.
2. 8 to 10 terms to recognize, one line each on why a customer cares.
3. Where the candidate's background lines up with published metrics or standards.
4. 3 topics they can discuss naturally without direct experience.
5. What to confirm, not assume.

Tone: a friendly colleague briefing them over coffee.

## Stage 2: Gap check

Compare the JD with the profile. Top 3 gaps, no more. For each: name it plainly in one sentence, list the true adjacent experience (never the same as the gap), give beats for talking about it, and state what not to claim. Lead with the strongest relevant work so the gaps land against a strength.

## Stage 3: Story bank

Pick 3 or 4 stories from the profile that fit the role. Default set: something that went wrong, ownership nobody assigned, learning fast under pressure, using a tool or data to change how a team worked.

Each story: 3 to 4 bullet beats, never a script. Say what it proves and what it does not. "I" for the candidate's decisions, "we" for team delivery. Every metric keeps its employer, denominator and time window. Flag any story whose ending is unconfirmed and say what to do if it cannot be confirmed in time.

Size: label STAR or SOAR, open with a one-line answer, two sentences of setup, one number, 60 to 90 seconds (about 150 to 220 words). Add a 30-second version.

## Stage 4a: Scorecard and question bank

**Scorecard first:** 5 or 6 competencies the JD signals most, each with what a strong and a weak answer look like. Anchors written before the mock keep grading about evidence, not polish.

**Three question banks.** Each question carries: why it will be asked (mapped to a JD line), what a strong answer shows (3 to 5 bullets), and the follow-up probe.
- Part 1: 6 to 10 JD-targeted questions, including one scenario with a constraint (deadline, angry customer, missing data).
- Part 2: 6 to 8 behavioral questions adjusted to seniority and culture.
- Part 3: deep-dives on the candidate's record, each labeled "high-risk probe" or "highlight to expand".

Do not show suggested answers before the mock; seeing them first defeats the practice.

| Gap in the answer | The probe |
|---|---|
| Ownership unclear | "What part of that was yours specifically?" |
| Low difficulty | "What made this harder than a normal week?" |
| Thin reasoning | "What else did you consider, and why did you rule it out?" |
| No result | "How did you know it worked?" |
| No learning | "What would you do differently now?" |
| All "we" | "Take me to a moment where you personally decided something." |

| Score | Evidence |
|---|---|
| 5 Strong | Demanding situation with constraints; owned the decision; says what was weighed; checkable outcome |
| 4 Solid | Clear ownership, specific actions, outcome stated; reasoning thinner |
| 3 Adequate | Real, relevant example; actions general |
| 2 Limited | Own part unclear, all "we", or no outcome |
| 1 Poor | Hypothetical, no example, or shows the opposite |
| IE | Insufficient evidence: a gap, not a low score |

Ready means 4.0 or higher on average, IE excluded.

## Stage 4b: Live mock

Stay in character as the interviewer type. One question at a time, wait for the answer, 5 questions with one follow-up where an answer is thin, including one scenario. No coaching or grading until the user types "done".

After "done": per answer, one thing that worked and one small fix. Score each on six delivery checks: answered first, one story, under 90 seconds, one number, stopped on the result, right altitude. Name the single delivery habit to fix. Then stop; do not re-run to chase a better score.

Morning-of variant: 6 short questions, 60-second answers, light feedback after each.

## Stage 5: Talking points and questions

3 talking points to raise naturally and 3 questions for the interviewer. Match questions to the interviewer: a recruiter can answer process, team shape and why the seat is open; save technical questions for the hiring manager or a peer. A good recruiter question: what the next interviewer tends to dig into.

## Stage 5b: Pay

Beats, not a script. Ask for the user's number or range and reasoning if the profile lacks it; never invent a figure. Beats: the number or floor, the reason, growth framing, flexibility. Acknowledge a posted range if there is one. Afterwards, note how the recruiter reacted, and do not read a polite reaction as acceptance.

## Stage 7: Final rundown

One scannable page, short lines, readable in a minute:
1. Stages covered, one line each.
2. MENTION: 5 things to say, unprompted if needed.
3. FILL IN: the 3 gaps with the honest one-liner for each.
4. AVOID: what not to say, including number swaps and names on the naming-rules list.
5. GLOSSARY: grouped, one line per term, only terms likely at this stage.

Tone: a colleague handing over a card before they walk in.

## Delivery rules for every answer

- Answer first, then the story. One story per answer.
- Spoken English: sentences under 25 words, specifics over adjectives.
- Once the point lands, stop talking and let the interviewer ask the follow-up.
- Match the lead to the interviewer: recruiter wants reliability and communication; hiring manager wants outcome, ownership, judgment; technical peer wants a concrete walkthrough.

## Working rules

1. One stage at a time.
2. Beats, not scripts. A memorized answer sounds memorized.
3. Scorecard before mock.
4. Stop when it is done. Over-studying costs more than it adds.
5. Glossary terms are reference, not recall.
6. Never swap a metric between employers.
7. Name gaps before they are found.
8. Check the company's live pages the morning of; published numbers move.
